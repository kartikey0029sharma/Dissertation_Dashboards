Decision-Making with Business Intelligence Dashboards

Newcastle University Business School  ·  MSc Business Analytics (ISO8007)  ·  Researcher: Kartikey Sharma

Participant information sheet

What is this study about? It is about how managers read the dashboards and performance reports they use at work, and how those screens shape the decisions that follow. It is not a test of your ability. There is no score, and no individual result will be reported to anyone.

What will I be asked to do?

You will read eight short business situations. Each shows a performance dashboard and a brief. You will choose what you would do and answer a few short questions about it, then answer some questions about your organisation. It takes about 15 minutes.

Do I have to take part?

No. Participation is voluntary. You can stop at any point by closing the page, and nothing you have entered will be kept. After you submit, you may ask for your data to be deleted up to two weeks later, after which the responses are anonymised and cannot be traced back.

Please do not disclose confidential information

Do not enter anything commercially sensitive: no client names, no internal figures, no screenshots of your own systems. Employers are recorded by sector and size band only.

How is my data handled?

  • Responses are stored in a spreadsheet held in the researcher's Newcastle University Google account, and are accessible only to the researcher and the supervising academic.
  • No name, email address or IP address is collected at any point. Your responses are anonymous, and the reference shown at the end is the only thing that links them to you.
  • Processing is on the basis of your consent, under the UK GDPR.
  • Results are reported only in aggregate, in an MSc dissertation and any publication arising from it.

Ethics and contact

This study has been approved under the Newcastle University Business School ethics procedure and follows the MRS Code of Conduct. Questions may be sent to k.sharma7@newcastle.ac.uk.

Thank you, and what this study was really testing

The scenarios were built so that the dashboard could mislead you. In each situation the screen pointed one way and the brief contained a piece of local knowledge pointing the other way. In four of the eight the screen also carried a notice that the underlying data were unreliable. In the other four the same fault was present but was not shown.

Whether the decision would be recorded against the system or recorded as your own judgement was also varied deliberately, four situations each way.

Following the dashboard was a reasonable thing to do. The study is not measuring whether you were right. It is measuring whether the conditions around a decision change how managers behave. Nobody is scored and nothing is reported at an individual level.
